A documentary credit is a bank's promise to pay against paper. Read the paper wrong and the bank pays for goods nobody shipped.

This is an evaluation lab built for that job: expert tasks, verified reference answers, and a grader tested harder than the models.

Every task is a real examination. One letter of credit, five or six documents, and a rulebook that states each tolerance the bank actually applies.

Twenty-three rules and twenty-six finding codes. The documents are seeded and synthetic, so every package rebuilds, and no real customer data appears anywhere.

The expected answer is not typed in. A rulebook interpreter reads the same rendered pages the model sees and computes the findings itself.

Each scenario also records what its author intended. When the engine disagrees with the intent, that is a benchmark bug, and the build refuses to finish.

Seven weighted dimensions grade the outcome, never the steps: recall, precision, rule mapping, evidence, severity, uncertainty, and format.

Then the hard caps. Miss a critical discrepancy, or invent one against a clean presentation, and the trial scores zero however well it was written.

The grader is validated against correct answers and deliberately wrong ones: one hundred and seventeen fixtures across the eight tasks.

Fifteen mutation levers prove the gate can fail. Break one rule and the suite must go red, and it is checked that it goes green again.

The platform is a FastAPI artifact store and a console. Tasks, transcripts and scores are served from files, with no database anywhere.

The integrity view lists the fixtures per task, the levers under test, and the coverage gaps that are disclosed rather than quietly dropped.

The transcript view puts a model's raw output beside the scorecard that graded it. That is where three grader bugs were found.

Nine models came from the providers' own listings, run at temperature zero, three trials per task, on free tiers only.

The first panel was wrong, and it was my harness. Hidden reasoning consumed the entire output budget, so models returned thinking and no answer.

One model averaged zero point zero zero. After the fix the same transcripts averaged seventy. The invalidated journal stays in the repository as the audit trail.

The completed panel: one hundred and sixty-eight scored trials, sixty-one point nine percent passing, best model seventy-two point four out of a hundred.

Rule mapping and severity sit just under ninety-eight percent. Recall is seventy-nine. Uncertainty is sixty-one.

So the models are not short of knowledge. They are short of noticing, and of saying that they cannot establish something.

The second and third most-missed findings are the minor ones: a variance the credit never stipulated. Models either ignore it or escalate it. The rule asks for neither.

One host is read-only for reviewers. Writes are refused at the edge with four-oh-three before they reach the application, and the mutating host sits behind zero trust.

Reproducing this needs no keys: install, validate, test. That same proof runs inside a container built from a clean clone.

Every number in the document is regenerated from the stored transcripts by one command, and a test fails if the writing drifts from the data.

The deliverable is not a set of prompts. It is a benchmark whose truth is computed, whose grader can be proven wrong, and whose numbers you can rebuild.
